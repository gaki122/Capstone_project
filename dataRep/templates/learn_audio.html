Audio & Sound

Discover how computers convert real-world sound into digital information that can be stored, processed and played back.

What Is Digital Audio?

Sound is produced by vibrations that travel through a medium such as air. Computers, however, need digital data to store and process information.

Digital audio is created by measuring a sound wave at regular intervals and converting those measurements into numerical values.

Important Audio Concepts

🌊

Sound Waves

Sound travels as waves. These waves contain information about the sound we hear.

📊

Sampling

Sampling measures the sound wave at regular intervals to create digital data.

🎚️

Bit Depth

Bit depth determines how precisely each audio sample can be represented.

What Is Sample Rate?

Sample rate describes how many times per second a sound wave is measured.

Example

A sample rate of 44,100 Hz means that the sound is measured 44,100 times every second. This is commonly associated with CD-quality digital audio.

What Is Bit Depth?

Bit depth determines how many possible numerical values can be used to represent each audio sample.

Example

With 8 bits, there are 256 possible values. With 16 bits, there are 65,536 possible values. Higher bit depth allows more precise representation of each sample.

How Sound Becomes Digital Data

The basic process can be understood in four simple stages:

1

Sound

A real-world sound is produced.

2

Sampling

The sound wave is measured.

3

Quantization

Measurements are converted into numerical values.

4

Binary

Numerical values are stored as digital data.
