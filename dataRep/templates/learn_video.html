Video & Digital Media

Learn how computers represent moving images, frames, colours and other digital media using binary data.

🎬 What Is Digital Video?

Digital video is a sequence of digital images called frames that are displayed quickly one after another.

When the frames are shown fast enough, our eyes perceive them as continuous movement.

▶

🎞️ Frames

A video is made up of many individual images. Each individual image is called a frame.

The frames are displayed one after another to create the appearance of motion.

Example: If a video contains 30 frames every second, it has a frame rate of 30 frames per second.

⏱️ Frame Rate

Frame rate is the number of frames displayed every second.

24 FPS

Commonly associated with cinematic video.

30 FPS

Common frame rate for television and online video.

60 FPS

Provides smoother motion and is common in games and sports.

🖼️ Video Resolution

Video resolution describes the number of pixels used to create each frame of a video.

A higher resolution generally means more pixels are used to represent the image.

720p

HD video resolution.

1080p

Full HD video resolution.

4K

Much higher resolution than Full HD.

🎨 Colour Representation

Each frame in a digital video is made from pixels. The colour of each pixel can be represented using digital values.

A common method is the RGB colour model, which uses red, green and blue components.

Example: A pixel can contain different numerical values for red, green and blue to represent different colours.

💾 How Is Video Stored?

A digital video contains a large amount of information. The computer stores this information as digital data.

Important: Video files can become very large because they contain many frames and each frame contains many pixels.

📦 Video File Size

Video file size depends on several factors, including resolution, frame rate, duration, colour information and compression.

Resolution

More pixels generally require more data.

Frame Rate

More frames per second can require more data.

Compression

Compression can reduce the amount of storage required.

🧠 Quick Summary

• Video is made from frames.

• Frame rate tells us how many frames are displayed each second.

• Each frame contains pixels.

• Pixels contain digital colour information.

• Resolution describes the number of pixels in each frame.

• Video files store large amounts of digital information.
